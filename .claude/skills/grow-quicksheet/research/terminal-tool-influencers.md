# Terminal-Tool Influencers & Signal-Boosters

**Date:** 2026-06-03  
**Goal:** Identify ≤10 accounts/people whose attention correlates with star spikes for terminal projects. Not a spam list — a "who to be visible to" reference.

---

## Summary (≤5 lines)

- **Newsletters** (Terminal Trove, Console.dev) are the easiest high-leverage channels — both accept submissions via form.
- **Charm.sh team** (Christian Rocha, Bashbunni) actively RT/boost novel TUI projects; they love seeing non-Go TUIs too.
- **Justin Garrison (rothgar)** curates awesome-tuis AND runs a YouTube review series — one listing = passive discovery.
- **ThePrimeagen** is the highest-reach individual for terminal/CLI content — one mention can spike hundreds of stars.
- **Scott Hanselman** is the .NET world's gatekeeper — one blog mention or tweet normalizes a .NET tool for the broader dev audience.

---

## The List

### 1. Terminal Trove (newsletter + directory)
- **What they do:** Curate and catalogue CLI/TUI tools. "Terminal Tool of the Week" feature. ~weekly newsletter.
- **Reach:** Thousands of subscribers; posts also go to Mastodon (@terminaltrove@mastodon.social).
- **Submission:** https://terminaltrove.com/post/ — requires PNG/GIF preview image, install instructions, description.
- **Why it matters for QS:** A listing here surfaces the project to exactly the right niche audience. The image requirement means we need a good screenshot first.

### 2. Console.dev (newsletter)
- **What they do:** Weekly newsletter reviewing interesting dev tools. 30k+ subscribers.
- **Submission:** https://console.dev/submit — tool name, URL, short description.
- **Why it matters for QS:** Broader than terminal-only, but they cover CLI tools. A feature here reaches a general dev audience.

### 3. Christian Rocha (@meowgorithm)
- **Who:** Co-founder & CEO of Charm (Bubble Tea, Lip Gloss, Gum).
- **Platforms:** Twitter/X, Mastodon (@meowgorithm@mastodon.social).
- **Pattern:** Boosts novel terminal UIs regardless of language. Appreciates design-forward CLI projects.
- **Visibility path:** Ship something visually distinctive → post about it → hope for organic RT; or participate in Charm community discussions.

### 4. Bashbunni (@thebashbunni)
- **Who:** DevRel / content creator, former Charm team. YouTube channel, Twitch.
- **Platforms:** Twitter/X, YouTube (youtube.com/bashbunni), Mastodon, Bluesky.
- **Pattern:** Features "cool terminal things" on streams and videos. Loves aesthetic terminal setups.
- **Visibility path:** A compelling screenshot/GIF shared in terminal communities may get picked up.

### 5. Justin Garrison (rothgar)
- **Who:** Curates github.com/rothgar/awesome-tuis. YouTube channel with TUI review videos.
- **Platforms:** GitHub, YouTube (@rothgar).
- **Pattern:** Accepts PRs to awesome-tuis; reviews notable additions in video format.
- **Visibility path:** Submit a PR to awesome-tuis once QuickSheet has a good README screenshot. His video "The best TUIs" features tools from the list.

### 6. ThePrimeagen
- **Who:** Popular developer content creator focused on Vim, terminal productivity, performance.
- **Platforms:** YouTube (1M+ subscribers), Twitter/X, Twitch.
- **Pattern:** Showcases interesting CLI/TUI tools he discovers. High engagement on "look at this terminal tool" content.
- **Visibility path:** Organic discovery (hard to pitch directly). Best angle: if QS goes viral on HN/Reddit, he may pick it up. The "spreadsheet as wallpaper" angle is novel enough.

### 7. Scott Hanselman (@shanselman)
- **Who:** Microsoft VP, prominent .NET blogger, podcast host.
- **Platforms:** Blog (hanselman.com/blog), Twitter/X, YouTube.
- **Pattern:** Periodically features interesting .NET community projects. His annual "awesome .NET tools" posts drive significant traffic.
- **Visibility path:** Tweet at him with a compelling GIF once the project has polish. His blog posts about interesting .NET tools are among the most shared in the ecosystem.

### 8. Jesse Duffield (jesseduffield)
- **Who:** Creator of lazygit, lazydocker — two of the most-starred TUI projects on GitHub.
- **Platforms:** GitHub, Twitter/X.
- **Pattern:** Occasionally shares TUI projects he finds impressive. His star on a project is a social signal.
- **Visibility path:** Being visible in the TUI space generally. A star from him validates the project.

### 9. sharkdp (David Peter)
- **Who:** Creator of bat, fd, hyperfine — beloved CLI tools in the "modern UNIX" movement.
- **Platforms:** GitHub (26k+ stars on bat alone).
- **Pattern:** Less of a "booster" and more of a quality signal — if he stars something, people notice.
- **Visibility path:** Organic. Be present in the same spaces (awesome-cli-apps, terminal-apps.dev).

### 10. agarrharr (Andrew Harrer)
- **Who:** Maintains github.com/agarrharr/awesome-cli-apps — one of the most-starred awesome lists for CLI.
- **Platforms:** GitHub.
- **Pattern:** Accepts well-documented PRs. Getting listed here = long-tail passive discovery.
- **Visibility path:** Submit PR once QuickSheet is polished. Must fit a clear category.

## Implications for QuickSheet

1. **Screenshot/GIF is the #1 blocker.** Terminal Trove requires it. Awesome-tuis PRs land better with one. Influencer RTs need visual bait. The user should capture a compelling desktop-mode screenshot showing extensions, sparklines, and live processes — that's the "wow" image. Queue this as a user action.

2. **Submit to Console.dev NOW (no image required).** The submission form only needs a URL and description. QuickSheet already has a good README. This is the lowest-friction high-reach action. Draft a submission description and queue for user to paste into the form.

3. **Queue awesome-tuis PR after screenshot lands.** The rothgar/awesome-tuis list is TUI-specific and Justin Garrison actively reviews additions. QuickSheet fits under "Productivity" or "Data" category. Draft a one-line entry + PR description.

4. **The .NET angle (Hanselman) is unique positioning.** Most TUI tools are Rust/Go/Python. Being .NET makes QuickSheet novel in that ecosystem. A tweet or blog mention from Hanselman reaches an audience that has never seen a "TUI spreadsheet" before. The pitch angle: "zero-dependency .NET 9 TUI that replaces your desktop wallpaper with a live spreadsheet."
